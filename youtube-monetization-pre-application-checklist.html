YouTube Monetization Pre-Application Checklist (2026)

Monetization guide · Updated 8 November 2026 · 8 min read · by the YTFixer Team

Your first application to the YouTube Partner Program is your easiest one. A first application is judged on the channel as it stands. A second one, after a rejection, is judged on whether you actually fixed what was flagged.

Most failed first applications fail on things the creator could have checked in an afternoon: an old compilation still public, an AdSense step half-finished, or a channel that looks like a collection of other people’s work. Work through this checklist before you hit Apply. Only YouTube decides applications.

Why the first application matters so much

After a rejection, you have limited options, and each one has a clock on it. You can appeal within 21 days, but a good appeal needs a channel already fixed. If you skip the appeal, or it fails, you can reapply after 30 days for a first rejection, and after 90 days for later rejections.

That is why the order matters: fix first, then apply. Reviewers re-check the channel, not your intentions. If nothing visible changed, the answer will not change either, and your next wait becomes 90 days instead of 30. From 1 February 2027, new applicants will need double the current thresholds.

The thresholds, in plain words

To apply now, you need 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million valid public Shorts views in 90 days. Both parts matter. The watch time must be valid and public, which means it must come from real viewing of your public videos, not from private videos, deleted videos, or anything bought or faked.

From 1 February 2027, new applicants will need 8,000 watch hours in 12 months or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days, alongside 1,000 subscribers. Channels already in the Partner Program are not affected. Do not close a small gap with bought subscribers, fake views, or purchased watch time. Those break YouTube’s policies.

Check your content first: reused and inauthentic

Reused content and inauthentic content are the two rejection reasons that surprise creators most, because both are judged on the channel as a whole. Reused content applies channel-wide, even with permission. It is separate from copyright: permission does not make the content yours.

Ask the honest question for every public video: is this meaningfully mine? A video passes when your voice, face, analysis, or transformation is the main thing the viewer gets from it. A compilation with a short introduction does not pass it. Sort your videos into clearly mine, transformed, risky, and pure re-upload, then remove or remake the last group.

Inauthentic content is the other test. It targets content that feels repetitious or mass-produced: the same template, the same structure, minimal variation. The policy was renamed on 15 July 2025. Add a recognisable human presence, real variation, and evidence of effort in each video.

Check your channel basics

Reviewers look at the channel, not just the numbers. Fill in your About section as a real creator would, and check that titles and thumbnails honestly match the video content. A title that promises one thing and a video that delivers another is a trust problem.

Check your metadata too. Misleading tags and trick descriptions do not help an application. Remove comments that advertise policy-breaking services. Make sure you have no active community guideline strikes, and sort out copyright claims or strikes first. Three strikes in 90 days can terminate the channel.

Check your AdSense readiness

AdSense is where many applications quietly stall, and it is the part creators leave until last. The rule is simple: one AdSense account per payee. A duplicate account, even an old forgotten one, can block approval. Check whether you already have one, and if you do, use that one.

Create your AdSense account only in YouTube Studio. Do not create it through a link someone sends you, and do not let anyone else create it for you. The name must match your official ID exactly. The address must be real, because your PIN is mailed physically. Identity verification must be readable, and your payments profile must be complete.

Two rules will save you trouble later. You can change the linked AdSense account only once per 32 days, so do not link the wrong account in a hurry. And never share your passwords. Nobody legitimate needs your Google password to fix AdSense.

The step-by-step fix: work in this order

  1. Confirm the thresholds honestly. Check in YouTube Studio that you have 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days.
  2. Turn on 2-Step Verification. Do this before you apply. It protects the account that holds your channel, your AdSense link, and your earnings.
  3. Sort your AdSense account. Confirm one account, in your real name, at a real address where you can receive post. Create it only in YouTube Studio.
  4. Audit every public video for reused content. Sort each video into clearly mine, transformed, risky, or pure re-upload. Remove or remake the last group.
  5. Audit the same videos for inauthentic content. If your videos all follow the same template, add your voice, face, own examples, and real variation.
  6. Fix titles, thumbnails, and metadata. Make sure every title and thumbnail honestly matches its video, and remove misleading tags and policy-breaking comments.
  7. Check strikes and claims, then decide your date. Confirm no active strikes, sort out copyright claims, and write down your key dates: 21 days to appeal, 30 days to reapply, and 90 days after later rejections.

The 12-point pre-application checklist

  • Thresholds genuinely met: 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days.
  • 2-Step Verification is on for my Google account.
  • AdSense: one account per payee, my real name matching my ID, my real address where I can receive post.
  • AdSense created only in YouTube Studio, with identity verification submitted and payments profile complete.
  • No active community guideline strikes on the channel.
  • Every public video passes the “is this meaningfully mine?” test.
  • No compilation or re-upload risks hiding in old videos; the whole channel has been reviewed.
  • Titles and thumbnails honestly match the video content.
  • No misleading metadata, tags, or trick descriptions.
  • About section and channel identity are filled in like a real creator’s channel.
  • Comments and pinned content do not advertise policy-breaking services.
  • I live where the YouTube Partner Program is available, and I can explain, on camera if asked, how I make my videos.

If you cannot tick every box yet

That is normal, and it is the point of the checklist. Do not apply anyway and hope the reviewer misses the gap. Work the list in order, starting with the content audit, because content fixes take the longest. Do not link the wrong AdSense account in a hurry: you can change it only once per 32 days.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need 4,000 hours or 8,000 hours?

Now, you need 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months, or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. From 1 February 2027, new applicants need 8,000 hours or 20 million Shorts views.

I have permission to use the clips. Am I safe from reused content?

No. Reused content is separate from copyright, and it applies channel-wide even with permission. Permission solves the copyright question. It does not make the content yours. You need significant original commentary, transformation, or educational value added by you.

Can I have more than one AdSense account?

No. The rule is one AdSense account per payee. A duplicate account, even an old forgotten one, can block approval. Create your account only in YouTube Studio, and never share your password.

How long do I have to appeal if I am rejected?

You have 21 days to appeal. If you skip the appeal, or it fails, you can reapply after 30 days for a first rejection, and after 90 days for later rejections or a failed appeal. Plan those dates on the day the rejection arrives.

What to do next

Print this checklist and work it in order. Start with the thresholds and the content audit, because those fixes take real time. Write down your three key dates before you apply: 21 days to appeal, 30 days to reapply after a first rejection, and 90 days after later rejections.
