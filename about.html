About YTFixer

Updated November 2026 · by the YTFixer Team

YTFixer is an independent help site for YouTube creators rejected from the YouTube Partner Program (YPP) — or who want to avoid rejection. We explain why YouTube said no, what to fix, and how to use your appeal or reapply window.

Who runs YTFixer

YTFixer is run by a small independent team. We built YTFixer because rejection emails from YouTube are short, vague, and hard to act on. This site turns those few words — “reused content” or “inauthentic content” — into a practical checklist you can work through.

What YTFixer does

We publish free guides on the most common YPP rejections, including reused content, inauthentic content, and AdSense problems. Our free eligibility checker asks eight quick questions and flags the risks holding your channel back. For creators who want a second pair of eyes, we offer paid written audits by email — $59, $99, or $149 depending on depth — reviewing your videos against the policy YouTube named and telling you what needs to change.

What YTFixer does not do

We are honest about our limits, because a false promise costs you your one appeal. YTFixer cannot guarantee monetization — only YouTube decides who joins the Partner Program. We do not sell subscribers, views, or watch time: buying them breaks YouTube’s policies and can end a channel. We do not buy or sell channels, and we never ask for your password. A written audit needs only your public channel link and the rejection message YouTube sent you. YTFixer is not affiliated with YouTube or Google.

How it works

Email us your channel link and the rejection message from YouTube. For a free guide, start with the article that matches your rejection and work through its checklist. For a paid audit, tell us which tier you want — $59, $99, or $149 — and we will reply by email with what the review covers. We look at your public videos only, explain what we found in plain words, and give you a fix list to follow before your appeal or reapply date.

The rules and dates to know

To join the YouTube Partner Program, a channel needs 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. From 1 February 2027, new applicants will need 8,000 watch hours or 20 million Shorts views instead. If YouTube rejects you, appeal within 21 days — and only if the channel is fixed. Otherwise, reapply 30 days after a first rejection, or 90 days after later rejections or a failed appeal.
