YouTube Shorts Views Not Counting Towards Monetization? The 90-Day Rule Explained

Guide · Updated November 2026 · About 8 min read · by the YTFixer Team

Your Shorts are getting views, but your monetization progress bar barely moves. A Short passes 50,000 views, you open YouTube Studio, and your YouTube Partner Program progress looks almost unchanged. It is natural to think YouTube is broken, or the views are fake. In most cases, neither is true. The views are real, but they are counted in a different window, on a different clock, and toward a different threshold than most creators expect.

What “not counting” usually means

“Not counting” usually means one of three things. First, the views are counting, but toward the Shorts threshold rather than toward watch hours, so the watch-hours bar does not move. Second, the views counted once and then dropped out of the 90-day window, so the total falls even while new views arrive. Third, the views are not eligible at all — because the Short was deleted, set to private, or the views came from a source YouTube does not count.

Work out which one you are seeing first. In YouTube Studio, go to Earn and look at the two separate tracks: 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months, or 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days, plus 1,000 subscribers for either track. If watch hours are flat while Shorts views climb, that is normal. If your Shorts total is lower than the sum in Analytics, that is usually the 90-day window.

The 90-day rule, explained in plain English

For the Shorts route into the YouTube Partner Program, YouTube counts only valid public Shorts views from the last 90 days. Not your all-time total. A rolling 90-day window that moves forward every day.

Think of it like a queue. Every day, the views you earned 91 days ago leave the queue, and yesterday’s views join it. Your total can therefore fall on a day when you gained views, if more old views expired than new views arrived. A channel with one viral Short three months ago and nothing since will watch its eligible total fall steadily, even though the video still shows a large public view count.

How the 10 million Shorts views threshold works

To qualify through Shorts, you need 1,000 subscribers and 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days. Both conditions must be true at the same time. Having 10 million views but only 800 subscribers does not qualify you, and having 1,000 subscribers and 9 million views does not qualify you either.

Ten million is enormous for most small channels, and that is the point: Shorts views are quick and cheap compared with long-form watch time, so the bar is set very high. A Short with 100,000 views in 90 days contributes 1 percent of the threshold. You would need one hundred Shorts at that level, in the same 90-day window, to reach 10 million. Only public Shorts count — views on Shorts you later delete or set to private do not count, and views on long-form videos do not count toward the 10 million either. You must be above the line at the moment YouTube checks your eligibility.

Why Shorts views don’t count as watch hours

Shorts views do not count toward the 4,000 watch hours needed for the long-form route. The two routes are alternatives, not a combined total. You cannot add 5 million Shorts views to 2,000 watch hours and call it halfway on both.

Five reasons your Shorts views look wrong

If your Earn total still looks lower than it should, work through these five causes in order.

1. Old views have expired. A Short that went viral 100 days ago no longer contributes to your 90-day total. Check views by date, not by lifetime total.

2. You are comparing the wrong numbers. The public view count, the Analytics number, and the Earn number are three different numbers. Only the Earn number is the monetization number.

3. Some views were filtered as invalid. Repeated plays by the same viewer, misleading titles, or artificial traffic can be filtered. YouTube does not itemise them.

4. The Short is no longer public. If you deleted a Short or set it to private, its views stop counting.

5. Studio data is delayed. Numbers update with a delay, usually within a day or two. Wait 48 hours before concluding views are missing.

How to check your real Shorts numbers in YouTube Studio

Do not rely on the public view count. Check the monetization number itself.

  1. Open YouTube Studio and click Earn. This is the only page that shows your progress toward the Partner Program thresholds.
  2. Read both tracks separately. Note your subscribers, watch hours in the last 12 months, and Shorts views in the last 90 days, with today’s date.
  3. In Analytics, filter by Shorts for the last 90 days. Compare that total with the Earn number. A gap is normal, because the two filter views differently.
  4. Look at views by day, not by video. Find the days when large numbers of views will expire soon.
  5. Check your top Shorts are public. One private or deleted viral Short can explain a large part of a missing total.
  6. Repeat the check weekly, on the same day. A weekly record shows whether your 90-day total is rising, flat, or falling — and that trend is what matters.

Step-by-step fix: make your Shorts count toward YPP

There is no button that makes old views count again. The fix is a steady flow of new eligible views inside the current 90-day window.

  1. Confirm your route: 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. Stop measuring yourself against the 4,000-hour bar.
  2. Work out your daily pace: 10 million divided by 90 is about 111,000 views a day. If you average 10,000 a day, the long-form route may suit you better.
  3. Keep every Short public. Do not delete underperforming Shorts — a Short with 5,000 views still contributes 5,000 views.
  4. Publish consistently. One viral Short is not a strategy, because its views expire after 90 days.
  5. Make Shorts people finish. Keep the opening second clear, deliver one idea per Short, and avoid misleading titles.
  6. Do not buy views, subscribers, or watch time. Bought views are filtered out, and buying engagement breaks YouTube’s policies.
  7. Grow subscribers alongside views. Views without subscribers do not qualify you.
  8. Re-check after 14 days. If the 90-day total is rising, keep going. If it is flat or falling, not enough new views are arriving.

What changes on 1 February 2027

From 1 February 2027, the thresholds for new applicants rise. The Shorts route will need 1,000 subscribers and 20 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days — double the current 10 million. The long-form route will need 1,000 subscribers and 8,000 watch hours in the last 12 months, up from 4,000 hours today.

Checklist: are your Shorts views on track?

  • You are measuring Shorts views in Earn, not the public view count.
  • You know your 90-day Shorts total and subscriber count today.
  • Your top Shorts are all public.
  • Your 90-day total is rising week by week, not falling.
  • You are averaging enough daily views to reach 10 million in 90 days — or you have honestly decided the long-form route fits better.
  • You have not bought views, subscribers, or watch time.
  • You know the 1 February 2027 change: 20 million Shorts views for new applicants.
  • You know your appeal window (21 days) and reapply windows (30 or 90 days).

Frequently asked questions

Do Shorts views count toward the 4,000 watch hours?

No. Shorts views count only toward the Shorts threshold of 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days. They do not add watch hours to the 4,000-hour target.

Why did my Shorts views total go down when I gained views yesterday?

Because the 90-day window moves every day. Views earned 91 days ago leave as new views join. If more old views expired than new views arrived, your total falls.

Do views on a deleted or private Short still count?

No. Only valid public Shorts views count. If you delete a Short or set it to private, its views stop counting toward the 10 million threshold.

Will my Shorts views still count after 1 February 2027?

Views will still be counted over the same rolling 90-day window, but new applicants from 1 February 2027 will need 20 million Shorts views in 90 days instead of 10 million.

Can I buy Shorts views to reach 10 million faster?

No. Bought views are filtered out, and buying views, subscribers, or watch time breaks YouTube’s policies.
